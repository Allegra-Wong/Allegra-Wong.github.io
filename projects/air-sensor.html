Air Quality Sensor

Overview

This Air Quality Sensor project was developed as part of the Ecolibrium program of Loisaida Inc., a non-profit organization located in Manhattan's Lower East Side. This organization promotes community-based sustainability projects at the intersection of culture, art, media, and technology. The Ecolibrium program focuese on climate literacy and environmental justice, examining local environment conditions with the goal of improving public health and quality of life within the neighborhood. As a part of this initiative, the air quality monitoring systems are intended to be deployed in local senior housing to provide insight into air quality conditions experienced by the residents.

The goal of this project was to design and fabricate an enclosure for the air quality monitoriung system, prioritizing easy of assembly, batch production, and maintainability. The system's electronics were also further developed to simplfy production. Throughout the summer, I worked on refining enclosure designs in OnShape, which was used for ease of collaboration. Through this project, I gained additional hands-on expeerience in rapid prototyping, laser cutting, and iterative design.

In addition to technical design work, I documented and communicated project developments through weekly progress presentations. This experience allowed me to strengthen my skills in mechanical design, collaborative work, and tehcnical communication while contributing to a community focused environmentalk initiative.

Lasercut Model

During my spring semester before the summer internship period, I worked on the initial laser cut model of the air sensor case. I further developed this design, working on improving assembly of the case. Utilizing The Cooper Union's equipment, I learned how to operate a laser cutter in order to fabricate the case pieces. The walls are cut from 1/8th inch acrylic sheets and held secure by 4 screws.

The objective of this model was to assemble 10 laser cut cases as proof of Loisaida's concept. This was achieved, but this model had several shortcomings, such as maintenance requiring complete dissassembly and lengthy assembly and fabrication time. Project funds allowed for the purchase of a 3D printer, which allowed for the transition from a laser cut model to a 3D printed model. This greatly expedited production and assembly time, and made maintenance significantly easier.

Early Concept Sketches for 3D Model

The earliest concept for the 3D printed case started with a simple box enclosing a shelf that would hold and organize the electronic components.

Exploded View of the Interior

All the electronic components are nested within a skeleton structure, which provides support and organization for all the components. This skeleton can then slide in and out of a protective and aesthetic case, allowing for easy access to the electronics for maintence and updates to the software and UI. All the components of the case utilize snap-fits, slide-fits, and dovetail joints, eliminating the use of screws or adhesives.

Top-Open View

In addition to the simplification of the casing design, the power board was also reselected from the original laser cut design to allow for direct connection between the power board with the battery and ESP, eliminating the need for USB-A and micro-USB connector cables. This in addition with the elimination of fasteners helped reduce the cost of the overall assembly and improved organization inside the structure.

Future Development

Future work will involve further development of the battery of the electrical system, as using a thermal camera built in Loisaida displayed significant heat around the battery. This suggests that prolonged use of the of the system could affect sensor accuracy and cause overheating within the structure.
